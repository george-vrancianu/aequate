## 4. Analysis (hand-written; facts first, recommendations labelled)

### 4.1 Facts from the run

- All 20 steps passed the parse stack (ANTLR strict, normalizer, whitelist, Lark cross-check). The Romanian inputs parsed as intended: `(4x+8):4` gave `(4*x + 8)/4` unevaluated (`x + 2` evaluated) and `\tg(x)\cdot\cos(x)` gave `cos(x)*tan(x)`.
- The ANTLR backend of SymPy 1.14 already builds unevaluated trees. The evaluation happens in `parsing._fix()` (its `xreplace` rebuilds the tree with evaluation on). `rawparse.py` re-runs the same steps under `sympy.evaluate(False)` (parsing.py stays verbatim), so every step has two parses, both shown in section 0. Evaluation at parse time erases domain information (`x/x` -> 1, `\sqrt{x}\cdot\sqrt{x}` -> x, `(4x+8)/4` -> x+2), so the S7 domain tracking runs on the unevaluated trees; the numeric and simplify strategies run on the evaluated ones.
- No strategy hit the 2 s timeout, and none raised. Everything on these 20 easy steps finished in tens of milliseconds, so the table says little about hard inputs. The tight-budget pass (sensitivity section above) shows timeouts appear once the budget is cut to 50 ms, which is the only evidence about timeout handling here. The kill check shows the worker is really killed at the deadline. Undecided only appeared as `UND` from `Expr.equals` (see below) and never from timeouts.
- The three expression-level "equal" strategies and numerics all agree on every non-domain step: S1 (simplify), S2 (equals), S3r/S3c (numeric sampling) and S4 (layered) gave identical verdicts on 19 of 20 steps in the default (complex symbols) setting. They all missed the same three domain traps (#7, #17, #19) and said EQ (3 wrong each).
- The three domain traps (#7, #17, #19) are caught by S7 (symbolic domain tracking: EQ_DOM, with the differing set named, e.g. "x = 1 defined after only") and, for the equation trap #17, by S5 (solveset: empty set vs {1}, reported as WIDER). S5 is NA on the two expression traps (#7, #19), because an expression has no solution set. S7 is no longer tied to a point grid: on the extras (not part of the 20, section 2) it also flags the off-grid `\frac{x^2-25}{x-5} -> x+5` (x = 5), and the evaluation-sensitive `\sqrt{x}\cdot\sqrt{x} -> x` (x >= 0 only) and `\frac{x}{x} -> 1` (x = 0). S1 says EQ on all three. S7 scores 17 correct / 2 partial / 0 wrong / 1 undecided on the 20 steps. The 0 wrong is evidence on 3 traps plus 3 extras that I picked, not a general guarantee. The 2 partial are the one-way steps (#14, #15), which S7 (residual meaning) reports as NEQ; the undecided one is #13: S7 handles one variable only (x, y here), a limitation.
- S7's domain rules are minimal: denominators of negative powers, tan/cot/sec/csc via cos/sin, even roots (>= 0, or > 0 if also in a denominator), log arguments (> 0), each solved with `solveset(..., S.Reals)` and compared as sets. The comparison of two `ImageSet` expressions (#19) works because both sides share the same `cos(x)` term; it would not recognise two differently-written equal sets. `continuous_domain` was not used (the reviewer found it returns Reals for `cos(x)*tan(x)`).
- Dropped-root and squaring steps (#11, #14, #15, #16) are told apart only by S5: NARROW (roots lost, a mistake) vs WIDER (extra roots, one-way squaring). The residual-proportionality meaning used by S0-S4/S6/S7 gives plain NEQ for both, so the ONEWAY steps score "partial" (rejected, but not recognised as one-way).
- Real vs complex: on the 19 steps other than #20, S3r and S3c agree, and S5r and S5c agree. The semantics only bites on #20 (`sqrt(x^2)` -> `x`). Under default (complex) symbols S1/S3/S4 say NEQ. With `real=True` symbols they still say NEQ. With `positive=True` symbols S1/S2/S4 say EQ. So "equal" for #20 exists only under an x >= 0 assumption, which is exactly the DOMAIN label.
- `Expr.equals` is randomised through SymPy's own RNG (not the hash seed). It is now seeded (`sympy.core.random.seed(0)`, `random.seed(0)`) in S2, so the main table is repeatable. Over seeds 0-9, `sqrt(x**2).equals(x)` (#20) gave NEQ 6 times and UND 4 times (#7 gave EQ 10 of 10), so the verdict on #20 depends on the seed (S2 is UND at seed 0). Undecided is a real outcome of S2. A cache keyed by (before, after, policy) should include the seed, or avoid S2 as a verdict source.
- Scoring was tightened after review: a plain NEQ on a DOMAIN step is now "partial" (it rejects a step that is valid under a restriction, rather than flagging the domain), and ONEWAY needs WIDER (NARROW is partial). #20 `\sqrt{x^2} -> x` is relabelled NOT_EQUIV (it is wrong for every x < 0 where both sides are defined; it is a different kind of step from the removable singularities). #17 is also one-way as solution sets (empty set implies {1}), so S5's WIDER there is not a mislabel.
- S1 never proves non-equivalence: a non-zero `simplify(A-B)` was recorded as NEQ here (that is what a naive checker would do), and in this set it was always right, but the detail strings (e.g. `-x + sqrt(x**2)`) show it is only "not shown equal".
- S0 structural, now both ways. S0e (evaluated trees) is right only on no-ops, reorders and cases the evaluation collapses: 9 correct, 5 partial, 6 wrong (the wrong are false rejects of correct expand/factor/trig/equation steps, which is S0 by design), and it says EQ on #5, #6, #18 because the evaluation made both sides the same tree. S0u (unevaluated trees, a true structural comparison) is stricter: 6 correct, 5 partial, 9 wrong; it says NEQ on #5 `(2x+4)/6` vs `(x+2)/3`, #6 and #18 (`(4x+8)/4` vs `x+2`), so those three S0e hits were produced by the evaluation. S0u is useful only as a "same form" check. All 6 of S0u's correct verdicts (and 6 of S0e's 9) are structural NEQs on the NOT_EQUIV steps, which says nothing about algebra; the 5 partials are the three DOMAIN steps and two ONEWAY steps rejected as plain NEQ.
- S6 (assumption variants): only #20 flips verdict (positive symbols give EQ). No other step is sensitive to real vs positive.

### 4.2 Limits of this evidence

- 20 hand-picked, mostly easy school steps; none stresses the timeout. No inequalities, systems, logs, mixed trig, floats, or parse rejections were exercised.
- "Equation meaning" for S0-S4/S6/S7 (residuals proportional) is this prototype's choice. Other meanings (STACK's numerator-ratio test) would behave the same here, but this was not tested.
- S7 is symbolic but limited: one variable, a short rule list (see 4.1), real policy only. No inequalities, no nested radicals of products, no multi-variable domains (#13 is UND). The extras that test it were chosen by me after the review.
- Timeout counts under the tight budget (section 2) are noise around a 50 ms threshold including fork overhead (changed between runs of the same code); the measured fork/IPC floor is in section 2. S2 does not use `failing_expression=True`, and S5 does not report "same roots" with multiplicity (no step exercises either); both are not done.
- Expected labels are mine; the reasoning is in section 0.

### 4.3 Recommendations (not facts)

Stacking order, cheapest first, stop at the first decisive answer:
1. Parse with the guarded stack; a Reject is "cannot check".
2. S3 numeric reject (real points, relative tolerance): fast (single-digit ms) and gave no false NEQ here. A NEQ is final for plain expression equivalence. For equation lines it is NOT final: the residual meaning gives NEQ for the dropped-root and squaring steps (#11, #14, #15, #16), so for equation lines run S5 before, or instead of, the numeric reject (or on every NEQ), otherwise step 4 never runs.
3. S0/S4 cheap zero-checks (expand/cancel/together/factor/trigsimp) then S1 simplify for a proven EQ. Treat `equals` (S2) as an optional last resort, since it is randomised.
4. For equation lines, S5 solution-set comparison over the stated domain is the decisive step: it is the only strategy that distinguishes mistake (NARROW) from one-way (WIDER) and caught the equation trap.
5. For every EQ, run the S7 domain flag on the unevaluated parse (never on the evaluated tree: `x/x` is already 1 there) and show amber, not green, when domains differ. Extend it beyond one variable and test it on more steps before trusting it.
6. Anything undecided or timed out (2 s looked ample here; consider a lower budget such as 500 ms for interactive use) shows as amber "can't check", never green or red.

### 4.4 Policy questions for "Decide CAS equivalence semantics"

- Domain: real or complex? In this run the choice only mattered for `sqrt(x^2)` vs `x`. Is the default real (school algebra) with a per-problem override, and is `positive` ever the default for formula-rearrangement tasks?
- A step equal everywhere except at a point (#7, #17, #19): green, amber, or red? Amber with the excluded value named ("only if x != 1") is what S7 enables. Also, `\sqrt{x^2} -> x` (#20, an assumption x >= 0, wrong otherwise) is labelled a mistake here: should it be red, or amber like a removable singularity?
- One-way steps (squaring): accepted as valid with a flag (S5 WIDER), or rejected? Should the checker demand the student check for extraneous roots later?
- Dropped root (NARROW) vs plain wrong: same red, or a specific hint? S5 can tell them apart; S1-S4 cannot.
- Equation equality: same solution set (S5), proportional residuals (S1-S4), or both? These agreed on every EQUIV step here but only S5 sees direction.
- Repeated roots: is `(x-3)^2=0` equivalent to `x=3`? (Extra x4: S5 says EQ because solveset loses multiplicity; the residual meaning says NEQ.)
- Undecided handling: amber with "can't check", retry with a longer budget, or fall back to numerics-only "probably equal"?
- Tolerance: numeric relative tolerance 1e-9 with 10 points worked here; should "probably equal" from numerics alone ever be shown green when symbolic proof fails or times out?
- Form vs equivalence: should expanded/factored/fraction-in-lowest-terms be checked (S0 shows structural difference exists), separate from equivalence?
- S2 `equals` nondeterminism: seeding makes it repeatable per seed, but the verdict on #20 changes with the seed (NEQ 6 / UND 4 over 10 seeds). Is the seed part of the cache key, or is S2 not used as a verdict source?
