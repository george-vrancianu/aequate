## 9. Findings in prose (facts; read the tables above for the numbers)

**Parse stack.**
- 31 of the 35 hard steps parsed (the set includes 3 deliberate parse probes, H33-H35). Rejected or hung: H22, H33, H34, H35. No whole category was rejected: inequalities (`<`, `>`, `\le`, `\ge`, `\leq`, `\leqslant`) parse to SymPy relationals, and `\begin{cases}` systems are split by `check_line` into one `Eq` per line (the harness's own `run.py` would have called a system "branches" and skipped it; `hardlib.py` keeps the lists).
- H22 `(0,5+x)^2`: rejected, "decimal comma inside parentheses" (intended behaviour of the normalizer: ambiguous with a list separator). Outside parentheses the comma is resolved to a point: `0,5x+0,25x` -> `0.75*x` (evaluated parse; the evaluated parse fuses the floats, the unevaluated one keeps `0.25*x + 0.5*x`), `2,5x=10` works, `0,1+0,2` -> `0.1 + 0.2`.
- H33 (probe) `x(x-2)=3`: rejected "undeclared function x": a variable directly followed by a bracket is read as a function call. This is the guard doing its job, but it is an everyday student form (`x(x+1)`, `2x(x-3)` too). The step set uses `x\cdot(x-2)` to get past it.
- H34 (probe) `a^2b^2` (any product of two powered factors): rejected "double superscript". The guard regex `\^\s*(?:\{..\}|\\?\w+)\s*\^` reads `^2b^2` as `^{2b}^2`. Braced exponents (`a^{2}b^{2}`) pass. The long expansion H26 uses braces for that reason.
- H35 (probe) `16\sin^5 x-20\sin^3 x+5\sin x`: the parse does not return in 10 s (measured separately: no result after 60 s, 3 variants incl. `\sin^{5}(x)`). The Lark reading is ambiguous (Lark parses `\sin^5 x-20\sin^3 x+5\sin x` with the whole rest as the argument of the first sin), and `parsing.same()` then runs `simplify(nsimplify(p)-nsimplify(q))` on the mismatching alternatives. `16(\sin x)^5-...` parses in 110 ms. **The parse stage needs its own kill timeout; it is not bounded by the CAS budget.**
- `\log` parses as the natural log in SymPy (`\log(100x)` -> `log(100*x)`, raw `log(100*x, E)`), while `\lg` is log10 (`log(100*x)/log(10)`) and `\log_{10}` / `\log_{2}` give `log(x)/log(10)`. So `\log(100x) -> 2+\log x` would be NOT_EQUIV after parsing even though a school student means base 10 there. H5 uses `\lg`.
- Parse time is the largest single cost of a check (see the tables): median of the four parses of one step 149 ms (hard) / 111 ms (original), max 464 ms. Of that the Lark cross-check is the biggest part (median 80 ms per step vs 33 ms for ANTLR strict; max 316 ms).

**Strategy findings on the hard set** (budget 2 s unless said):
- Nothing timed out at 2 s in 51 steps x 16 strategies x 3 reps (0 timeouts). The slowest cells: `Expr.equals` (S2) on H17 half-angle (1.2 s) and H18 sin 5x (0.75 s; both end as UND, not as an answer); S6-S4r/S6-S4p about 410-430 ms of CAS time on H6 (logarithmic equation, ends UND); S6-S1p and S6-S2p about 450 ms on H31 `(x^{10}-1)/(x-1)`; S6-S2r/p up to 1.2 s on H17. Every other cell is below 200 ms (S4 up to about 180 ms CAS on H17/H18; S7 up to about 140 ms).
- Timeouts appear only at 500 ms and below, and only in the S2 family: S2, S6-S2r and S6-S2p time out on H17 and H18 at 500 ms and 200 ms (the strategy table gives 2 each, 3 for S6-S2p at 200 ms with H28 added); at 100 ms they also lose H28, and S4, S6-S1p, S6-S4 and S7 start to lose a few cells (the table in section 5 lists them).
- S1 (simplify) cannot prove the two harder trig identities: `sin 5x` and the half-angle formula give NEQ (non-zero residual, "not proven"); S4 gives UND; only S3 numeric says EQ. S2 is UND or too slow. So for trig identity steps of this size nothing in the cheap stack proves equality: the stack ends in UND for H17 and H18 (amber).
- S7 (domain tracking) gave a false EQ_DOM on H17: `\frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x} -> \tan\frac{x}{2}` have the same excluded set, written as one `ImageSet` and a `Union` of two; this is the "equal sets written differently" limit predicted in RESULTS.md 4.1, now observed. S7 is also UND on steps with two or more variables (H1 `\ln(xy)`, H26 `(a+b+c)^4`, O13) and on variable-free steps (H20, H27, H28) because it implements exactly one variable, and on H6 (its numeric check is UND). In the stack, two or more variables is reported as EQ_NODOM (proven equal, domain not checked), and no variable is treated as EQ.
- H6 (log equation, ONEWAY): S5r and S5c both return `{-1, 3}` for before (`\ln x+\ln(x-2)=\ln 3`), the same as for after, so they report EQ: SymPy's `solveset` does not drop the extraneous root -1 of a logarithmic equation (checked directly: `solveset(log(x)+log(x-2)-log(3), x, Reals)` = `{-1, 3}`). S5 is therefore not decisive for log equations; the ONEWAY label is only reached by hand.
- Logarithm domain traps: S1/S2/S4 (complex or real symbols) say NEQ on `ln(xy) -> ln x + ln y` and `ln(x^2) -> 2 ln x` (rejected, "partial" for a DOMAIN step); S3r numeric says EQ (wrong) because only points where both sides are real count, and for H2 only the positive ones; the positive-symbol variants S6-*p say EQ (wrong, they assume the restriction). S7 flags H2 (EQ_DOM) but is UND on H1 (two variables). `ln(x^2) -> 2 ln|x|` (H3, correct) is called NEQ by every complex-domain strategy (principal branch of log of a complex number) and only S3r, the real-points numeric check and the positive-symbol variants say EQ. The default real/complex semantics decision therefore decides this step, not the algorithm.
- Floats: `0,1+0,2 -> 0,3` (H20): S1 says NEQ (`simplify(A-B)=2.7756e-17`, a float residue) and S2 says False; S3 numeric and S4's numeric stage say EQ (S4 ends UND). S5 on `2.5x=10 -> x=4` (H23) compares `{4.0}` with `{4}` and reports NARROW (wrong): a `Float` in a solution set is not equal to the Integer. `\frac{x}{3} -> 0,33x` (H25, a rounding mistake) is NEQ in every strategy. No tolerance policy is exercised (S3 uses a fixed relative tolerance of 1e-9, which would not accept 0.33 for 1/3 either).
- H28 `\sqrt[3]{2+\sqrt5}+\sqrt[3]{2-\sqrt5} -> 1`: SymPy's principal cube root of the negative number is complex, so S1, S2, S3c, S4 say NEQ (wrong for the school reading, the real cube root) and S3r (real points; there are no free symbols, so no point is "usable") is UND.
- Nested radical (H27 `\sqrt{3+2\sqrt2} -> 1+\sqrt2`), the cyclotomic factoring `x^{12}-1` (H29), the five-fraction sum (H30) and the (x+1)^8 and (2x-3y)... expansions (H24, H26) were all proven by S1, S2 and S4 in under 60 ms CAS time. The stressors that are expensive are not the algebraic ones (high-degree factoring, the large rational function) but the trig identities (H17, H18). The quintic H32 `x^5-x-1=0 -> x^5=x+1` did not blow up: `solveset` returns `{CRootOf(x**5 - x - 1, 0)}` for both sides over the reals, equal sets, EQ in about 56 ms.
- Inequalities (via the sign-aware wrapper in `hardlib.py`): sign-flip mistake H7 NEQ in all strategies (correct), H8 EQ (correct); the ONEWAY steps H9, H10 are NEQ ("partial") for the residual-based strategies and WIDER (correct) for S5. S0 on inequalities and systems compares structure only. Systems: only S5 (solution sets, `sympy.solve`) and S0 exist (the other strategies are N/A); S5 was right on all three systems (H11-H13) at 12-18 ms.
- Fork/IPC floor: a no-op through `isolated()` takes about 3 ms (median, 200 runs alone; the same inside the 4-way parallel sweep). A warm long-lived worker (S0e round trip, parsed step pickled) is about 0.5 ms; over all strategy cells that finished in both modes the median wall time went from 9 ms (fork per check) to 2 ms (warm worker), a saving of about 6 ms per call, with identical verdicts. Killing a warm worker costs a re-fork (18 over 3 reps x 816 calls at 500 ms). The floor is small against the parse (150 ms) and the S1/S4 tails (up to 180 ms).
- Parallelism: the same sweep run serially (one worker, 500 ms budget only) gave a median strategy-cell wall time of 10.6 ms vs 11.5 ms with 4 workers, p95 67 vs 70 ms, and a stack median of 160 vs 171 ms; so running 4 workers inflated times by roughly 5-8 %. Other agents on the machine were not controlled.
- Stack end to end (section 7): at a 2 s budget on the hard set: 21 correct, 1 partial, 3 wrong, 10 not decided (4 parse rejects or hangs; H26 as EQ_NODOM; H1, H6, H17, H18 and H20 as UND). H20 is UND because S4 cannot prove the float step (S1 sees a 2.8e-17 residue). The 3 wrong are H3 (complex semantics), H23 (Float in `solveset`), H28 (principal cube root); none of them is a speed problem. Uncapped end-to-end wall time (fork + 4 parses + stages): median ~175 ms, p95 ~400 ms, max ~560 ms on the hard set; CAS stages alone: median 21 ms, p95 118 ms, max 136 ms. At a 500 ms total budget one step times out (H30, the five-fraction sum, whose parse alone is 450 ms); at 200 ms half the parsed hard steps time out and at 100 ms most do, in every case because of the parse, not the CAS.

**Limits.** 35 hand-picked steps; labels are mine. One machine under variable shared load. Times are wall times of a killable child, so each includes about 3 ms of fork/IPC. The stack is my reading of RESULTS.md 4.3 (S5r first for equation lines; S3r then S4, with S7 on proven EQ), and 4 parses per step (evaluated and unevaluated, before and after) are more than a product would need. S7 and S5 for systems and inequalities are prototype-only adaptations (`hardlib.py`). No strategy was tuned to these steps.

## 10. Recommendation (not facts)

1. **Interactive budget.** On this machine the whole check can be about 500 ms of CAS time after the parse, and about 1 s end to end including parse and fork (the observed end-to-end maximum was 0.56 s; keep roughly 2x headroom because the machine was shared). 2 s is not needed by anything in the recommended stack: its slowest CAS stage was 136 ms. 200 ms end to end is not reachable with the current parse stack (parse median 150-170 ms with four parses) but is plausible for the CAS stages alone.
2. **Fix or bound the parse before tuning CAS.** The parse stage is the dominant cost and the only unbounded one (H35 never returns). Give it its own kill timeout (about 300-500 ms), parse each side once (not four times), and treat the Lark cross-check (about half the parse time) as an asynchronous second opinion rather than a gate on the first answer.
3. **Drop from the interactive path:** `Expr.equals` (S2: slowest, randomised, UND on the hard trig identities) and the S6 assumption variants (positive-symbol variants hide domain traps and have the slowest tails; they add nothing the real-numeric check does not). S0 (structural) is a "same form" hint only, never a verdict. S1 alone is not enough (it cannot prove two ordinary trig identities and says NEQ on floats).
4. **Run synchronously:** the guarded parse, S5r (equation/inequality/system lines, at most about 60 ms), S3r numeric reject (at most about 10 ms), S4 cheap zero-checks (tails up to about 180 ms), S7 domain flag (at most about 140 ms). **Defer asynchronously and upgrade amber to green later:** anything that ends UND from S4 on a trig identity (run a heavier prover with a larger budget, up to 2 s, off the interactive path), the Lark cross-check, and any multi-variable domain check once S7 supports it. Timeout and UND show as amber "cannot check yet", never green or red.
5. **A warm worker pool is optional.** It saves about 6 ms per call (the fork floor is about 3 ms) and gave identical verdicts; it matters little against 150 ms of parse. It becomes worthwhile only if parse and CAS stages move into the same long-lived worker (killed and replaced on timeout) to avoid re-importing the parsers.
6. **Inputs for "Decide CAS equivalence semantics" (not decided here):** the real-vs-complex default decides `\ln(x^2) -> 2\ln|x|`; Float handling (`0,1+0,2` and `2.5x=10` need rational conversion or tolerance before any strategy compares them); logarithmic equations need an explicit domain filter on top of `solveset`; multi-variable domain tracking and equal-set comparison (H17) for S7; the parse guards that reject common student writing (`x(x-2)`, `a^2b^2`, decimal comma in brackets); `\log` = natural log in SymPy versus the school reading.
